# GFS — reply to the supervisor, 11 August 2026

Supersedes `docs/gfs_update_for_supervisor.md`, which was drafted after Phase 3
and quotes a **crude proxy measurement**: the sum of past gauge rain and expected
forecast rain, rather than the trained model. Phase 4 re-ran the test properly
against the real model and the numbers below are those. Do not send the old

Short version for us, before the letter: **GFS has been in the system since

## The reply

> Dear Ajarn,
>
> Thank you — and yes, GFS is already in the system. It went in after your
> earlier suggestion, and we have since measured it properly against the trained
> model rather than a proxy. Here is where it stands.
>
> **1. It is integrated, in three places.**
>
> The features `rain_fcst_1h`, `rain_fcst_3h` and `rain_fcst_6h` are built from
> GFS (via Open-Meteo, `gfs_seamless`) at all 50 Bangkok district centroids. They
> are among the 50 inputs of the deployed onset model — the one that predicts
> flooding on roads that are currently dry. The live collector we started running
> this week fetches the same GFS fields hourly, so training and serving use the
> same source.
>
> **2. What it is worth, measured.**
>
> Onset model, 15 cm, one hour ahead, on the three cross-validation folds whose
> training years contain GFS:
>
> | | Onset recall | Precision | **Floods caught** |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | with GFS | **23.9%** | 14.8% | **65.1%** |
> | without GFS | 21.4% | 21.0% | 58.3% |
>
> So GFS buys about **7 more floods caught per 100, at a cost of 6 points of
> precision** — roughly one extra false alarm per two extra catches. We have kept
> it, but that trade is properly BMA's to make rather than ours, and we would
> like to put it to them in those terms.
>
> **3. On resolution, and the 1 km downscaling.**
>
> Your note is right about the resolution, and the consequence is larger than it
> first appears. Compared against the BMA gauges over 1.38 million district-hours,
> the GFS forecast correlates with observed rain at **0.014 on wet hours**, with a
> 16% hit rate. It can tell that the region is wet. It cannot tell which district.
>
> We also measured the extreme case: a model given GFS, calendar and terrain and
> **nothing else** catches **1.6 floods in 100**, at 0.2% precision.
>
> This is why we would not recommend investing in downscaling GFS rainfall to
> 1 km. Downscaling adds resolution; it does not add information. It works well
> for air temperature — which is exactly the case in your note — because
> temperature has a strong, stable relationship with things we already know at
> 1 km: elevation through the lapse rate, land cover, urban fraction. A
> downscaling function has something real to key on.
>
> Convective rainfall over Bangkok has no such predictor. The city varies by only
> a couple of metres of elevation across its whole width, so there is no
> orographic signal to exploit, and the storm cells that cause our floods are
> 2–5 km across and form in places GFS cannot resolve at all. We would produce a
> 1 km rainfall map whose fine detail was invented, and the invented detail would
> look convincing on a screen. That seems to us a worse outcome than an honest
> coarse field.
>
> **There is a version of this we would be glad to do.** Statistical downscaling
> needs ground truth to train against. BMA's 131 rain gauges are exactly that
> ground truth. If we can get a live feed from them, correcting GFS against the
> gauge history — per district, per season — becomes a well-posed problem with
> something to calibrate to. Without the gauges there is no target, and any 1 km
> product is a guess with a fine grid.
>
> **4. On the dashboard — a fair point, and no.**
>
> GFS is inside the model but it is not visible on the dashboard. The monitoring
> panel currently plots observed BMA gauge rainfall, canal level and canal flow;
> there is no forecast trace. We will add one, showing predicted rain alongside
> observed rain for the selected district, so the divergence between them is
> visible rather than buried in the model. It is a small change and we think you
> are right that it should be there — partly because it makes the system's
> weakest input the easiest one to inspect.
>
> **5. What would move the number most.**
>
> For context on where GFS sits among our constraints. Running on everything
> publicly available today, the system catches about 5 floods in 100. Running on
> BMA's own archive it catches 53. The gap is measured rainfall, and the ranking
> is:
>
> | Adding | Floods caught per 100 |
> |---|---|
> | public data only, today | 5 |
> | + live BMA rain gauges | ~43 |
> | + live canal level and flow | ~85 |
>
> GFS is worth keeping and we are keeping it. But no amount of work on GFS —
> downscaling included — reaches the first row of that table. A live tap on the
> gauge network does.
>
> Happy to walk through any of this, and to show the forecast-versus-observed
> panel once it is in.
>
> Best regards,
> Satoru

## Notes for us, not for sending

**The supervisor's note appears to be written for the Heatwave Early Warning
project** — it discusses predicting air temperature and heat-risk levels. That is
worth knowing because it explains the 1 km framing: downscaling to 1 km is
genuinely standard and genuinely works for temperature. The reply above treats
the advice as correct in its own context rather than as a mistake, which it is.

**The earlier draft was never sent, or was not seen.** The supervisor is asking
the same question a second time. Whichever it is, sending this one promptly and
by a channel they will read matters more than its contents.

**Numbers and where they come from:**

**Two limitations the reply does not raise**, because they weaken it without
changing the decision — mention them if asked:

- The GFS archive starts 23 March 2021, so cross-validation fold 1 trains with no
  forecast at all.
- GFS goes only to the onset specialist. Adding it to the general model made
  results worse: it helps where a forecast is needed and adds noise where the
  current sensor reading already answers the question.
- PR-AUC actually *drops* slightly with GFS (0.109 → 0.097) while event POD
  rises. Both are true; event POD is the operational metric, PR-AUC rewards
  ranking on rows that are mostly dry.

**The dashboard change we just promised.** Add forecast rain to
`serving.observations()` and plot it against observed rain in the frontend
monitoring panel. Small, and now committed to in writing.
