### Why the onset column is the one that counts

Persistence reaches 100% recall on rows where the road was already
flooded and 16% where it was dry. A single recall number averages
those into something that describes a monitor, not a forecaster.

## Events and lead time (15 cm, 3 h)

| baseline    |   events |   events_detected |   event_pod |   median_lead_minutes |   p25_lead_minutes |
|:------------|---------:|------------------:|------------:|----------------------:|-------------------:|
| persistence |      129 |             77.25 |       0.574 |                  15   |                 15 |
| rain_rule   |      129 |             83    |       0.637 |                  22.5 |                 15 |

An event is a run of **observed depth at or above the tier** and lead time is
measured from the moment the water arrived. An earlier version defined events
from label runs and reported ~1% POD with a 150-minute median lead; both were
artefacts. See `phase3_findings.md`.

A median lead of 15 minutes is one modelling time step: real warning, but not
enough to dispatch on. Closing that gap is the point of Phase 4.

## Promotion rule

A model is only worth deploying if it beats **persistence on onset recall and
event POD at the 15 cm tier**. Beating it on overall recall means only that it
has also learned to repeat the current reading.
